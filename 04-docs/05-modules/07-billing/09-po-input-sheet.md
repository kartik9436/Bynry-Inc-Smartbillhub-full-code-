### OQ-089 — A bill is issued and immutable, and its document cannot be rendered

**Question.** Issue is atomic and one-way: the invoice is immutable (BR-BIL-004), its template version is pinned forever (BR-BIL-026), and the customer owes the money. Rendering happens **after** issue, in the delivery queue, and the renderer belongs to notifications while the queue row belongs to billing (`07-pre-session-model.md:218`). The queue's status vocabulary — `queued \| sent \| delivered \| failed \| bounced` (+ `returned`, BR-PAY-018) — describes **provider** outcomes only. Nothing describes a **render** failure: a template variable that resolves to nothing, an empty `custom_snapshot` (US-BIL-028), a PDF engine error, a pinned version that was published with a defect.

Three things are undecided:
1. **What state does the row rest in**, and is the failure distinguishable from a provider failure on the per-run dashboard? Retry ×3 with backoff against a deterministic render failure is three guaranteed failures.
2. **Who owns the fix** — billing owns the queue row, notifications owns the renderer, and neither epic claims the failure.
3. **How is it repaired at all?** BR-BIL-026 guarantees the bill renders its pinned version *forever*. Publishing a corrected template version therefore does **not** reach the stuck bill. Under the rules as written there is no lawful repair path: the invoice cannot change, and the pin cannot change.

**Why this needs a ruling rather than an Eng decision.** Any repair either weakens BR-BIL-026's reproducibility guarantee (allowing a supervised re-pin) or moves the check before issue (a pre-render gate, matching the existing "issue fails atomically if a line cannot resolve a GL" precedent at `EP-BIL-02-invoice-gl.md:50`). Both are product rulings with audit and evidentiary consequences — a bill's rendered document is what the utility can produce in a dispute.

**Eng recommendation.** Pre-render at issue as an atomic precondition (option c in Part 2 #17): a bill does not issue unless its document renders. It reuses a pattern already ratified for GL resolution, keeps BR-BIL-026 absolute, and moves the failure to the only moment when it is still cheap — before immutability starts. Add a distinct terminal render-failure state on the queue anyway, for defects that survive the gate.

**Blocks.** The delivery-queue status enum and the issue-time transaction boundary (US-BIL-005, US-BIL-023). **Routed to.** Product Owner + Eng-Lead (BR-BIL-026 is a PO-ruled rule, Lane B B-7).

### OQ-090 — A service left out of a run's scope has no path to ever being billed

**Question.** US-BIL-001 lets the operator scope a run to a subset of services via the prototype's service chips, and states that an unchecked service is *"an explicit scope choice recorded on the run, never a silent skip"* (`EP-BIL-01-bill-run.md:36`; TC-4 at `:51` asserts the gas services are *"excluded by explicit scope, recorded — not reported as missing-read exceptions"*).

But the run idempotency key is **(tenant, cycle, period)** with no scope term (BR-BIL-005; DB `UNIQUE (tenant_id, idempotency_key)` at `02-database-design-brief.md:422`), so a second run for the same cycle and period to pick up Gas is **refused** (T5-05 proves this refusal is absolute, including the "just the stragglers" case at step 4). The only ratified door for stragglers is the catch-up sub-run, whose scope **must be a subset of the parent's held set** (US-BIL-006 TC-6, `:361`) — and scope-excluded accounts were never held.

So the ruling that an out-of-scope service is "recorded, never a silent skip" is true of the record and false of the outcome: it is recorded, and then it is never billed, with no queue row, no aging, no escalation and no report.

**Why this needs a ruling rather than an Eng decision.** The two candidate fixes are (i) auto-create Pending-billing queue rows for scope-excluded service/account pairs at run close, or (ii) add scope to the idempotency key. Option (ii) changes BR-BIL-005's **stated** key and a 🔒 key mechanic; CLAUDE.md's prime directive forbids an implementing agent from choosing it.

**Eng recommendation.** Option (i). It reuses the Pending-billing queue exactly as designed for holds, needs no key change, and makes the scope choice visible and ageing rather than merely recorded. The same mechanism resolves the parallel gap for accounts **excluded at the draft step** (Part 2 #3) — one rule, two failure modes closed.

**Blocks.** Run-close behaviour and the exclusions/queue seam (US-BIL-001, US-BIL-004, US-BIL-006). **Routed to.** Product Owner.

### OQ-091 — v1 delivery reality: paperless customers receive no document until W6

**Question.** OQ-073 closed with customer email dispatch **deferred → W6**: queue rows are written from issue day one, but there are *"NO ESP dispatch attempts"* before W6 (`EP-BIL-06-templates-delivery.md:162`). The story's own fixture splits run 07-A into **982 email / 142 print** of 1,124 bills (`:160`) — 87% on the deferred channel.

The closure ruled the **architecture** and the **timing**. It did not rule what a paperless customer actually receives in the interim. Meanwhile every downstream money mechanism runs normally on those accounts: due dates are set at issue (BR-BIL-024), the late-fee clock starts from the due date (BR-BIL-001/008), aging accrues, and the collection sequence fires (BR-PAY-017). J17 asserts explicitly for the returned-mail case that *"BALANCES CONTINUE NORMALLY — due dates, late fees, aging unaffected"* — the same is structurally true here, but for the majority of the book rather than one account.

**The decision.** Either (a) v1 renders print/PDF for **every** account regardless of the paperless preference, recording the preference and honouring it from W6; or (b) paperless customers genuinely receive nothing until W6, and this is stated to the tenant in writing at onboarding as a known v1 limitation. (c) — pulling email into v1 — reopens a closed ruling and is out of scope for the sheet.

**Why this needs a registry row.** It is a consequence of a **closed** OQ that the closure did not address, and it is a live-deployment issue of exactly the class the hard-won laws were added for (law 3: defaults fail closed; law 6: diff the running app against the prototype). It also has a compliance edge: several US states require that a bill be delivered before disconnection proceedings, and the collection sequence is currently indifferent to whether the document ever left the building.

**Eng recommendation.** Option (a) — print is the fail-closed default; a customer who preferred email getting a paper bill is a mild annoyance, a customer getting nothing is an unbillable month. Pair it with a delivery-channel report so the tenant can see the substitution.

**Blocks.** `US-BIL-023` channel resolution and the W3 go-live readiness statement. **Routed to.** Product Owner (+ legal, for the disconnection-notice interaction with BR-PAY-017).

# runbooks — Business Rules

**Family:** `BR-RBS-###` · **Pattern:** `BR-RBS-\d{3,5}(?!\d)`. ⚠ **The module code and the
screen series are BOTH `RBS`** (PO ruling 2026-09-06, decision log): a screen is `RBS-004`
while a rule is `BR-RBS-004`, a story `US-RBS-004` and a test `TC-RBS-004`. A search for a
SCREEN needs the lookbehind as well — `(?<![A-Z]-)RBS-\d{3,5}(?!\d)` — or it returns rules.
Searching for a RULE, the `BR-` prefix does that job. **Zero-padded to three, the spelling
chosen at birth** because this series carries no citations yet; padding one that already has
them is what law 11 forbids.

Format: `BR-XXX-###` · rule · source (spec §) · status · test ref.
Rules CITE their source; they never copy a value out of a signed document (one source of truth).

**Status vocabulary.** `CONFIRMED` cites a ruling or a signed document · `PROPOSED` cites the
derivation and is ours to decide and write down · `OPEN` cites the question that blocks it and
**may not be answered by a lane**.

**Source.** *Runbooks — business & engineering specification*, v0.1 draft, 4 September 2026,
restructured 5 September, build sheets 6 September; read against product `2.128.2`. Its **B6**
tables all thirty-nine rules below. Two companion documents were read with it: *Twenty-One
Runbooks* (the definitions) and the market study. **Nothing in B6 has been ratified by the
Product Owner** — the specification's own masthead says *"It decides nothing that is the
Product Owner's to decide"* — so every row here is `PROPOSED` except the one that restates
platform law already signed.

**What the PO HAS ruled about this module.** ⚠ **THIS PARAGRAPH READ *“decision log 2026-09-06,
four rows … none of those four touches a rule below”* UNTIL 2026-09-06, AND BOTH HALVES ARE NOW
FALSE** — the PO has ruled repeatedly about this module since, and **six of those rulings write a
rule.** Corrected rather than deleted, because a reader who met the old sentence would otherwise
conclude that nothing below carries a PO signature.

**The four that touch no rule:** the section is called **Runbooks**, a coined word, and *Check*
and *Action* are the two kinds on screen · the screens are **`RBS-001`…`RBS-008`**, their own
series · the 60,000–80,000-account figure on the market page is an illustration and the ICP stays
at 40,000 · and the nine market-page rulings are recorded split by who ruled each.

**The six that do**, all 2026-09-06: the default cap is **100** (`OQ-RBS-1731`, `BR-RBS-006`) ·
**every** money-bearing action carries the second approval and there is **no threshold**
(`OQ-RBS-1732`, `BR-RBS-018`) · closing the accounting period stays **a named person's act**
(`OQ-RBS-1736`, `BR-RBS-030`) · a Check **may cause a note**, through the engine (`BR-RBS-040`) ·
permission is granted **up front, per runbook** (`BR-RBS-041`) · and **a runbook is one fixed
process with declared inputs; a tenant sets input values and never arranges steps**
(`BR-RBS-042`, `OQ-RBS-1735`).

live.** Its appendix carries them as `RBR-01`…`RBR-08`: every step listed before the first one
starts, with a reason on any that did not run · Stop reachable at every moment of a batch ·
